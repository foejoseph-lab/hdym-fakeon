# Path B step 1: the one-mode fakeon projection (30 Oct 2026)

Working notes for `fakeon/toy/`. Status: step 1 of brief 10 path B done for the harmonic and single-wall cases
to order τ¹⁴ (K = 7); reviewed once by a reader who had not seen the work, findings folded in (History).
Physics reading and whether to carry it to Bianchi IX are the author's call.

## Question

Anselmi's classical fakeon projection (1809.05037 §4; 1901.09273 for FLRW) replaces the interim fourth-order
equation of L = (m/2)(q̇² − τ² q̈²) − V(q) by the nonlocal second-order equation m q̈ = −V′(⟨q⟩),
⟨q⟩ = (1 + τ² d²/dt²)⁻¹ q with the half-retarded, half-advanced kernel G_F(u) = sin(|u|/τ)/(2τ). On a
gravitational background the projection is carried out order by order in τ² = 1/m_χ² (⟨q⟩ = q − τ²q̈ + τ⁴q⁗ − …,
each q^(2k) reduced along the flow), and Anselmi reports that this expansion is exact for special FLRW sources and
asymptotic for dust. Before Bianchi IX: does the order-by-order projection converge on a base flow with a bounce,
and what sets the boundary?

## Answer

1. **Harmonic base (single frequency ω): convergent, radius ωτ = 1/2.** The reduced equation at order K is
   q̈ = −Ω_K² q with Ω_K² = Σ_{k≤K} C_k τ^{2k} ω^{2k+2}, C_k the Catalan numbers — the Taylor series of
   Anselmi's (4.13), Ω² = (1 − √(1 − 4τ²ω²))/(2τ²), verified symbolically to K = 6 (`series_harmonic.log`).
   Its radius of convergence, ωτ = 1/2, is the point where the physical root Ω and the "fakeon-minus" root
   Ω′ = √((1 + √(1 − 4τ²ω²))/(2τ²)) collide and go complex, and the projected solution space becomes empty
   (Anselmi's "thick fakeons"). The toy analogue of the ABP20 condition m_χ² > H²/4 (2005.10293 eq. 4.9). The
   term ratios of this convergent series also grow with K (1, 2, 2.5, 2.8, … → 4): growth of the ratio is not
   by itself a divergence criterion; boundedness of the ratio is.
2. **Bounce off a wall (continuous spectrum): asymptotic, not convergent.** For V = V₀ e^{(q − q_w)/w} alone,
   one bounce of a unit-speed particle (V₀ = ½ puts the turning point at q_w; launched eight widths out, run
   eight widths back; τ = 0.2), the observable is the bounce time delay, dq_K = |q_K − q_{K−1}| at t_end (the
   outgoing-velocity differences agree where resolved and hit machine precision by K ≈ 5). The ratios
   r_K = dq_K/dq_{K−1} scale as c_K (τv/w)² with c_K universal in τv/w up to ≈ 0.3 and **unbounded in K**:
   c₂…c₇ = 1.00, 1.60, 2.35, 3.29, 4.47, 5.92 at τv/w = 0.1, increments 0.60, 0.75, 0.94, 1.18, 1.45,
   *increasing* (`series_wall.log`). A convergent series has bounded c_K with decreasing increments; the natural
   convergent candidate, the local-harmonic radius at the turning point (V″ = V₀E/w² = 1/(2w²), so
   ω_loc τ = (τv/w)/√2), would give c_∞ = 2, which c₄ already exceeds. The series is asymptotic, with optimal
   truncation where c_K (τv/w)² ≈ 1: **seen directly at τv/w = 0.5**, where dq_K shrinks to K = 5
   (8.9e-2 → 1.7e-3), dips at K = 6 (3.5e-4) and the K = 7 term is 55× larger (2.0e-2); at 0.6 the dip is at
   K = 5 (1.2e-3), K = 6 is 1.1e-1 and the K = 7 equation is stiff enough to exhaust the evaluation budget
   (recorded as a failure); at 0.4 the dip appears at K = 7. At τv/w ≤ 0.2 no truncation is reached by K = 7
   (dq₇ ≈ 1e-9 at 0.1). This is Anselmi's dust result in one degree of freedom, with the cause visible: a
   transient has spectral content at all frequencies, including at the kernel pole 1/τ, where the local expansion
   of (1 + τ²∂²)⁻¹ has zero radius.
3. **The naive fixed-initial-data iteration of the nonlocal equation does not contract** on a window of several
   periods, at ωτ = 0.1 or 0.3 (`picard.log`): each iterate adds polynomial-in-t terms (the Lindstedt problem),
   growing like (ωT)² per step, and the undamped kernel carries grid-edge content inward. The nonlocal line
   equation also admits the Ω′ solutions, so two initial data do not fix its solution for the harmonic case (not
   established here for nonlinear V); the projection *is* the restriction to the physical pair, which the
   order-by-order reduction performs automatically. Direct solvers of the nonlocal equation are the wrong tool
   (recorded in `fakeon_toy.py`'s docstring; a Newton–Krylov attempt, not committed, converged only at ωτ = 0.1
   and became ill-conditioned as Ω′ → Ω).

For Bianchi IX, as a heuristic only: τ ↔ 1/M and the bounce rate v/w ↔ the inverse proper duration of a
Taub-wall bounce, which for the wall ∝ e^{−8β₊} at Kasner speed is ≈ 8H up to O(1), so τv/w ≈ 8H/M × O(1) and
the squared geometric factor is ~64. The number of usable orders at a given H/M is therefore not to be read off
the toy; it needs the Bianchi I + one-wall reduction (Open, first item). What does carry over: the projection on
a bounce is an asymptotic series with an optimal truncation that shrinks as the bounce sharpens, so the question
for Mixmaster is "at what H/M does the optimal truncation drop to one or two orders", not "where is the radius of
convergence" — the same situation as ABP20's bound, which is a reality condition on the kernel, not a convergence
statement. Anselmi's dust result (asymptotic 1/(m_φt)² series, coefficient 10⁸ at order 14) is the same
phenomenon; a periodic base flow does not show it.

## Numerics

Reduction in sympy over the polynomial ring (q, p, E, τ²) with the wall exponential E as a generator
(dE/dq = E/w); with w substituted numerically before the reduction, K = 7 takes 15 s per wall width (symbolic w:
×8 per order, 143 s at K = 5). Integration DOP853, rtol 1e-13, atol 1e-15, with a terminal event at
|q|, |p| > 10³ and an evaluation budget of 2×10⁶ (a runaway or stiff iterate is a failure, not a long
integration). All values kept signed; the complex Ω for ωτ > 1/2 is printed as such. Rows at τv/w = 0.05 reach
the tolerance floor by K = 6–7 (dq ≈ 1e-13); the 0.1 row is the one to quote for c_K. The kernel check
(`check.log`) verifies the regularised Fourier multiplier against the principal value to 1e-6 away from the pole
and the τ → 0 limit on a Gaussian to O(τ²).

## Open

  with the Hamiltonian constraint reduced alongside, giving c_K and the optimal truncation directly in H/M. The
  step-2 entry point and the first gravitational case with C ≠ 0.
* Growth law of c_K (K^α fit, or log dq_K = a K log K + bK + c against aK + c) from K = 8–9; the Borel sum, if
  wanted, is then the natural definition of the projected bounce.
* Two walls (a billiard) and a Kasner-like free flight between them: does the asymptotic character compound
  bounce over bounce, or reset?
* The K = 6 dip before the K = 7 explosion at τv/w = 0.5 (and K = 7 at 0.4): a sign change in the correction,
  or a near-cancellation? Uninvestigated.
* Anselmi–Calcagni 2510.05276 initial-data counting on the wall problem: their limit prescription for
  a → −∞, b → +∞ should reproduce the order-by-order solution where that converges.
