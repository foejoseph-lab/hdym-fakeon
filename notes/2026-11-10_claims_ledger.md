# Claims ledger: what each paper set out to show, and by which methods it has been shown here (10 Nov 2026)

Generated by `notes/claims/build_claims_grid.py` from `methods.csv`, `claims.csv`, `cells.csv` — edit those, not this file.
Marks: **X** the method was carried through and its answer can be trusted; **X\*** carried through, the method failed and was
superseded for it; **S** superseded before it was run; **O** to do; **N** does not apply. A mark is about the method. Whether the
paper's claim held is the **verdict** beside each paper in section 2. Papers in order of publication throughout (author, 10 Nov).
"Method P" and "method M" are not used here: they name Feynman parameters with the average continuation and momentum-space
residues in `bubble/`, and were reused in rung 3 for DeWitt's recursion and the (3.40) formula.

## 1. By paper


**Pais & Uhlenbeck 1950** — source: memory

- `PU-1` propagator: the fourth-order oscillator splits into two modes, one of negative norm or energy

**Utiyama & DeWitt 1962** — source: abstract only

- `UD-1` one-loop divergence, matter on a classical background: exactly three orders (quartic, quadratic, logarithmic)
- `UD-2` one-loop divergence, matter on a classical background: the quartic order is a cosmological-term counterterm
- `UD-3` one-loop divergence, matter on a classical background: the quadratic order is a renormalization of G
- `UD-4` one-loop divergence, scalar on a classical background: the logarithmic order is quadratic in curvature (coefficients of R^2, Ric^2)
- `UD-5` propagator: the semiclassical graviton Green's function falls as 1/p^4

**Sakharov 1967** — source: memory

- `SA-1` one-loop divergence, matter on a classical background: the quadratic order determines G (induced gravity)

**Lee & Wick 1969** — source: second-hand

- `LW-1` pair bubble: defined by a deformed energy contour
- `LW-2` S-matrix: the complex pair contributes no imaginary part (unitarity)
- `LW-3` pair bubble: Lorentz invariant with the Lee-Wick contour and real spatial momenta

**Cutkosky, Landshoff, Olive & Polkinghorne 1969** — source: second-hand

- `CL-1` pair bubble: coalescing singularities resolved by a mass-shift limit, giving a definite value

**Nakanishi (Feb) 1971** — source: second-hand

- `NA-1` pair bubble: not Lorentz invariant with the Lee-Wick contour and real spatial momenta (the threshold moves with the frame)

**Lee & Wick (reply) 1971** — source: second-hand

- `LR-1` pair bubble: Lorentz invariance restored by complex spatial momenta plus the CLOP rule

**Nakanishi (June) 1971** — source: second-hand

- `NB-1` pair bubble: the Lorentz breaking is intrinsic to a Hamiltonian formulation with complex energies

**Gleeson, Moore, Rechenberg & Sudarshan 1971** — source: second-hand

- `GM-1` pair bubble: closed form at nonzero spatial momentum

**'t Hooft & Veltman 1974** — source: read in full

- `HV-1` one-loop divergence: master formula for a general second-order operator (3.40)
- `HV-2` one-loop divergence: the Riemann-squared term is never needed (Gauss-Bonnet, App. B)
- `HV-3` one-loop divergence, scalar on a classical background: the logarithmic order is quadratic in curvature (coefficients of R^2, Ric^2)

- `HV-8` one-loop divergence: pure gravity, off shell (5.24)

- `HV-12` S-matrix: independent of the choice of basic fields, Jacobian one in dimensional regularization (p. 70)
- `HV-13` one-loop divergence, scalar on a classical background: adding (1/12) R phi^2 removes the R^2 term (p. 79-80)
